Brazil's electricity grid

Brazil runs one of the largest interconnected grids in the world by area - the Sistema Interligado Nacional (SIN), covering over 98% of the country's electricity supply - and one of the cleanest by generation mix, thanks to a huge hydroelectric fleet built up over the 20th century.

No live figures on this page yet - see below

About Brazil's grid

Brazil's SIN is coordinated by the Operador Nacional do Sistema Elétrico (ONS), which balances output from a hydro fleet so large it can smooth out seasonal rainfall variation across river basins thousands of kilometres apart, complemented by a fast-growing wind and solar sector (especially in the north-east) and a smaller thermal (gas, coal, biomass) fleet used mainly during dry spells when reservoir levels run low.

The SIN also includes the Itaipu Dam on the Brazil-Paraguay border - one of the largest hydroelectric plants ever built, and jointly operated by both countries - part of why Brazil's grid is closely linked with several of its South American neighbours (see the Americas interconnections page).

Context

  • Hydropower share of generationAround half to two-thirds in a typical year, one of the largest hydro fleets in the world
  • Itaipu Dam14GW installed capacity, jointly operated with Paraguay - one of the largest hydroelectric plants ever built
  • Renewable share of the overall electricity mixAmong the highest of any large grid worldwide (hydro, wind, solar and biomass combined)
  • Sistema Interligado Nacional (SIN) coverageOver 98% of Brazil's electricity supply, coordinated by ONS

Sources: ONS (Operador Nacional do Sistema Elétrico), IEA Brazil country profile. Last checked: 23 August 2026.

Why there are no live figures here yet

Every other country on this site (Great Britain, Ireland, the rest of Europe, the USA, Canada) has a live, keyless or free-registration API this project has actually wired up - ENTSO-E for Europe, the EIA for the USA, IESO for Ontario. Brazil's operator, Operador Nacional do Sistema Elétrico (ONS), does publish an open data portal, but this project hasn't yet built and tested a parser against it. Wiring it up would mean writing and testing a new parser against a live response, the same work that went into each of the other countries' pages - not done yet for this one.

Rather than show a fabricated number here, or nothing at all, this page instead gives real, sourced background on Brazil's grid and points directly at where the real-time data actually lives.

Where to see Brazil's live grid data

  • ONS Dados Abertos (Open Data Portal) - Brazil's official open data portal for the national grid - generation, load and hydrological data, published by ONS.
